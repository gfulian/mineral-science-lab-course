Assignment 5 — Quasi-harmonic thermodynamics of MgO

Laboratory 5 brings together almost everything developed in the previous activities. You will not run the underlying CRYSTAL phonon calculations during the laboratory: a validated multi-volume dataset is provided because the \(4\times4\times4\) phonon calculations are too expensive for a teaching session.

Your task is instead to understand what was calculated, why those calculations are needed, and how they become finite-temperature and finite-pressure properties.

Before starting, read Laboratory 5 — From harmonic phonons to finite-temperature properties.

Scientific questions

The laboratory is organized around four questions:

  1. Why does a quasi-harmonic calculation require phonons at several volumes, including expanded volumes?
  2. How does the choice of free-energy minimization — polynomial or equation of state — affect the derived properties?
  3. Which calculated quantities can be compared directly with experiment, and under which thermodynamic conditions?
  4. What can we learn when calculated and experimental absolute values differ but their trends agree?

Start the laboratory

Update your personal workspace:

update-course

then create your Laboratory 5 working copy:

start-lab 05

Work only inside:

work/lab05/

The laboratory contains three notebooks:

01-understand-qha-dataset.ipynb
02-qha-minimization.ipynb
03-qha-properties-experiment.ipynb

They should be completed in this order.

The supplied calculation series

The instructor provides seven CRYSTAL phonon calculations spanning

\[ V/V_\mathrm{ref} = 0.91,\;0.94,\;0.97,\;1.00,\;1.03,\;1.06,\;1.09. \]

Do not treat these files as a black box. The first notebook asks you to inspect the input and output files and identify, among other things:

  • the phonon supercell;
  • the number of atoms in that supercell;
  • the number of q-points sampled;
  • the DFT functional and basis set;
  • the sampled volume interval.

A central point of the assignment is to explain why the series must extend to \(V>V_\mathrm{ref}\). Hint: think about what happensby heating a material.

Quasi-harmonic analysis

You will use Quantas to build a normalized multi-volume phonon input, inspect its pressure coverage, and calculate quasi-harmonic properties.

Two representations of the free-energy curve will be compared using exactly the same first-principles dataset:

polynomial minimization
versus
BM3 equation-of-state minimization

The objective is not to decide that one method is universally “better”. You will examine which physical quantities are sensitive to the numerical representation and whether both methods lead to the same scientific interpretation.

Comparison with experiment

The workspace includes documented experimental/reference data for MgO. You will compare calculations with:

  • standard entropy \(S(T)\);
  • constant-pressure heat capacity \(C_P(T)\);
  • enthalpy increment \(H(T)-H(298.15\,\mathrm K)\);
  • cell volume and thermal expansion at ambient pressure;
  • adiabatic bulk modulus \(K_S(T)\);
  • room-temperature compression \(V(P)\).

Experimental/reference points are plotted in black so that they remain visually distinct from the different computational models.

Particular attention should be paid to two volume plots:

  1. the absolute cell volume \(V(T)\);
  2. the normalized volume \(V(T)/V(T_\mathrm{ref})\).

You will look for any systematic offset between theory and experiments, but also check if the calculated trends agree with the reference data.

Final interpretation

At the end of the laboratory you should be able to distinguish:

numerical representation error
        from
first-principles model error
        from
limits of the QHA itself

Do not judge the calculation by the number of matching decimal places. The scientific objective is to decide which behaviours, derivatives and trends are reproduced and why.
